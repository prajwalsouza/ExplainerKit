Camera choreography and screen backdrops

Name regions of a scene, schedule camera shots, and add a steady camera drift when you need it. Existing camera.to, follow, frame and zoomTo keep their behavior and defaults.

On this pageOwnership and unitsContinuity and covering a shapeUnderlaysBounds-relative linear gradientsRegion-scoped content
scene.region('overview', {x: 0, y: 0, width: 960, height: 540});
scene.region('detail', {x: 2000, y: 1000, width: 480, height: 270});
const camera = scene.camera().drift({pan: [-15, -8], zoom: -.015});
camera.shots([
  {at: 0, frame: 'overview'},
  {at: 3, zoomThrough: star, duration: 1, ease: 'launch'},
  {at: 4.1, cut: 'detail', start: 'bottom'},
  {at: 4.3, frame: 'detail', duration: 1.4},
  {at: 7, whipPan: 'overview', duration: .9},
]);

Ownership and units

scene.region(name,box) registers or replaces a captured world-space rectangle. Register names before authoring shots; references to unregistered names fail with CAMERA_REGION. There are at most 128 regions and 64 shots. Inline rectangles are also accepted. Frames use uniform screen-pixel padding.

camera.shots(list) validates the complete list and authored endpoint views before replacing the prior choreography. Rules must be chronological and nonoverlapping. frame, cut, whipPan and zoomThrough are the recommended shot forms. region/to and cutTo are also accepted names. An initial frame at time zero takes effect immediately; other frames default to 1.1 seconds and whip pans to 0.9 seconds. Only cuts and the initial frame have zero duration. Edge start values place the camera center at that region edge, intentionally cropping the opposite half.

Shots own all pose channels. Mixing them with existing tracks, bindings, follow or legacy framing refuses with CHANNEL_OWNED. clearShots() releases the new list; clearFraming() retains its existing meaning for legacy frame/zoomTo rules. drift() is an explicit additive layer compatible with manual tracks; clearDrift() removes it.

Drift pan is in world units/second, rotation in radians/second, and zoom is fractional change per second. zoom:-.015 means multiply by 0.985 each second, implemented through integrated log1p(-.015). Zoom remains subject to the existing supported camera envelope; extreme rates or long times refuse rather than silently clamp. Per-shot drift overrides smoothly change velocity over that shot's duration; the next shot without an override returns toward global drift.

Continuity and covering a shape

A shot interpolates zoom logarithmically so magnification stays positive. It also smooths progress before applying the selected easing, which makes base camera velocity zero at transition boundaries while drift continues. This differs from applying a launch curve directly. Finite-difference tests check continuity of position and velocity, called C1 continuity, for supported named curves with static endpoints. Cuts deliberately jump. Custom easing functions must map 0 to 0 and 1 to 1 and preserve the required continuity between them.

Endpoint compensation removes accumulated drift before interpolation and reapplies it afterward, so a declared frame or cover is exact at its endpoint. Live zoom targets are sampled at the current time; earlier live targets freeze at the next shot's start. This is pure reevaluation, with no previous frame state. A moving target may require additional model samples during evaluation of past shot boundaries; callbacks remain trusted synchronous author code.

zoomThrough supports live circles and plain rectangles, including nested rotation, reflection, skew and nonuniform scale. It maps the viewport corners through the inverse scene.locate() transform into the actual local circle/rectangle and computes the required covering zoom. It does not claim coverage from an enlarged world bounding box. Rounded rectangles, groups, paths, collapsed/missing targets and screen-space targets refuse explicitly. Coverage is geometric; a transparent target does not become opaque. Motion blur and lighting remain separately authored.

camera.shotReport({width?,height?,samples=33,fps=30}) reports each rule's sampledPeakPxPerSecond and sampledPeakPxPerFrame, measured by projecting stationary world points initially at viewport corners. These are finite sampled diagnostics, not certified maxima or object-motion measurements. Cuts/initial establishment report null speed. Work is bounded by the scene/shot/sample product; the report does not run automatically during playback.

Underlays

camera.underlay(handle) places a live root handle in screen space below the projected world. Overlays remain above it. Model order/identity stay intact; only camera projection changes draw layers. Underlay and overlay assignments are mutually exclusive (CAMERA_LAYER_OWNED); use removeUnderlay/removeOverlay before changing the assignment. A backdrop can bind to explicit scene inputs like any other handle. It cannot be a camera follow or zoom-through target.

Tests in tests/core/camera-shots.test.js independently sample actual target geometry, endpoint poses, derivatives, ownership and backward seeking. tests/browser/camera-shots-checks.html checks actual full-viewport target pixels and fixed backdrops on SVG and Canvas. Run node benchmarks/camera-shots.mjs for a saved local report. These original fixtures do not include reference pixels or establish the reference-film camera-score gate.

Bounds-relative linear gradients

linearGradient({units:'bounds', x1:0, y1:0, x2:1, y2:1, stops}) from explainerkit/graphics places coordinates in the painted shape's own geometric 0–1 box. Existing gradients retain local user-space coordinates by default (units:'user' is explicit). SVG uses objectBoundingBox; Canvas maps the gradient normal through that box, preserving diagonal gradients on non-square rectangles rather than merely scaling the two endpoints. Zero-area boxes paint no gradient, matching SVG. Positive boxes below the supported precision or mapped coordinates that collapse/nonfinite refuse before drawing (paint.bounds.precision), including gradient masks. Native Canvas text requires an imported/explicit outline for bounds gradients and otherwise refuses with RENDER_UNSUPPORTED, capability paint.bounds.text, before drawing. Radial gradients keep their existing user-space contract.

The checks in tests/browser/glyphs-gradients-checks.html compare rectangles of different sizes and positions at one viewport size, including unequal scales. The recorded local Chrome run measured interior invariance error of at most 1/255 and SVG/Canvas mean channel error of 0.040/255. Antialiasing can differ at transformed edges, so the invariance check excludes a two-pixel border while the renderer comparison includes the whole frame.

Region-scoped content

scene.region(name,box,{content:group,visible:'inside'}) associates a live world-space top-level group with a world rectangle. Visibility uses exact convex rectangle intersection with the camera viewport, including camera rotation; touching edges count as intersection. visible:'approach' expands each viewport edge by 25% of its screen width/height before intersection. This margin is in logical viewport units and does not depend on DPR. Content is hidden outside the region's active view by multiplying effective group opacity by zero; inside, authored opacity and all other animation remain intact. It appears immediately at the boundary; this API does not add an implicit fade.

Without a camera, the stage viewport is the active view. sampleWorld() retains authored visibility for geometry/authoring inspection. A content group belongs to one region, remains a top-level world group, and cannot be adopted as an overlay or underlay. Re-registering a region without content removes its visibility association. Region boxes are world-space metadata and do not follow the group's own transforms. Camera target fitting uses the existing named box unchanged.
