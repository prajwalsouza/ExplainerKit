Held props and edge turnarounds

Attach a prop to a character's hand and turn a character at a chosen time. Both use the solved pose and sampled handle effects, without a separate attachment animation loop.

On this pageSocket orientation and prop holdingAuthored edge turnarounds

Socket orientation and prop holding

const hand = figure.socket('handF', 1); // {x, y, rotation}, world coordinates
const grip = figure.hold(prop, {
  hand: 'F', from: .4, to: 1.8, blend: .08,
  offset: [0, 0], anchor: [0, 0], align: 'hand',
});
// grip.dispose() removes this hold and exposes the prop's authored motion.

Sockets provide world position and rotation. Fixed part sockets follow that part's local x direction. Hand sockets in the bean/stick rigs declare a direction part; the last forearm segment or curve tangent supplies their orientation. Custom rigs can declare sockets.name.direction:{part:'armPart'} rather than depending on a specific limb name. Parent transforms, facing and the character's animated root are included. A socket describes the authored named endpoint; it does not infer an undeclared anatomical grip.

hold takes a live prop from the same scene. Its local anchor (default origin) lands on the socket plus offset. align:'hand' rotates both the prop direction and offset with the socket; upright keeps world rotation zero and interprets offset in world axes. The helper inverts the prop's actual parent matrix, so independently moving, reflected, skewed or scaled prop parents are supported. Its own scale/skew/pivot and non-transform style remain authored.

from defaults to zero. Omitted to uses the existing scene duration only when that leaves a positive window; otherwise INVALID_HOLD requests an explicit end. No implicit day-long scene is authored. The scene extends through the short release blend when necessary.

release:'freeze' (default) retains the release pose after to and its blend. Use release:'authored' to end the hold's transform ownership at to + blend and resume the prop's existing tracks or bindings. Earlier attachment history remains seekable. For a seamless throw, author the resumed path to start at the sampled hand/anchor release pose; the hold does not infer a trajectory or rewrite authored tracks. dispose() still removes the entire hold.

The short entry blend begins at from; after from+blend and through to, the anchor follows the socket exactly. The optional release blend ends at to+blend, then the prop stays at the socket's world pose sampled at to, including when its parent later moves. This frozen pose is a sampled effect, preserving arbitrary backward seeks. Blend defaults to at most .08 seconds, bounded by half the window and the timeline end. Zero blend is an exact immediate grip/release. Disposing the controller explicitly restores underlying authored transforms.

A prop can have up to 128 nonoverlapping hold windows. Front and back hands can hold it in sequence once the previous release blend has ended. Attaching a held glyph to a line is checked against those windows before allocation. An overlap is rejected with CHANNEL_OWNED, while a later attachment ends a completed frozen hold at its arrival start without changing earlier seeks.

Dependencies that would form a hold cycle, including ancestor/descendant holds and geometry-dependent ancestor pivots, report HOLD_CYCLE. A parent transform that cannot be inverted reports HOLD_SINGULAR. Screen overlays, underlays and depth layers are outside the world-space hold model and report HOLD_SPACE_UNSUPPORTED, including when their assignment changes after installation. The helper does not guess a conversion between world and screen coordinates.

Authored edge turnarounds

figure.keys([
  {at: 0, facing: 1},
  {at: 1, facing: -1, turn: {duration: .12, style: 'edge'}},
]);

A pose key’s at time is its arrival, so the turn in the example runs from .88 to 1 second. The character narrows horizontally, changes facing halfway through, and widens into the mirrored pose. Its horizontal scale stays at least .001 of its authored width so that coordinate conversion remains possible.

At the narrowest point, limb details not attached to the root bone fade behind the central silhouette. This prevents front and back details from crossing visibly through the torso. Their displayed sides exchange as facing changes. The method turns a cutout rather than reconstructing a body in 3D or creating profile artwork. Its endpoints match an instantaneous facing flip.

A turn requires a facing key, a positive duration fitting before its arrival, style:'edge', and no overlapping edge windows. Existing keys without turn retain their instantaneous behavior. The 120 Hz automatic biped planted-foot solver cannot keep world-fixed feet while horizontal body scale approaches zero; combining it with an edge turn explicitly refuses TURN_FOLLOW_UNSUPPORTED. Use follow(...,{feet:'none'}) plus authored foot targets for that sequence. Ordinary gait clips and existing instant-facing automatic-foot tracks remain available; a believable turning walk still requires authored choreography.

Original native checks exercise grips throughout every biped action, transformed prop parents, both hands, release/rewind, attachment ownership and every starter's edge endpoints. tests/browser/character-part-six-checks.html measures rendered marker centers and turnaround ink in SVG and Canvas; it reports numerical error and the number of starter/renderer pairs. These are synthetic correctness checks, not a claim that every turnaround is artistically convincing.
